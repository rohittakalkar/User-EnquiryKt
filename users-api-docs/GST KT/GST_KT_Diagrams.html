Trust & Compliance

GST — Goods & Services Tax

When a supplier submits or edits a GST number, a full verification process runs behind one text field: format & checksum checks, a lock that stops edits to already-verified GSTs, an external Government/BI verification call, automatic PAN extraction and legal-status derivation, a nightly re-verification cron, and a separate GST ↔ HSN tax-classification mapping flow.

Business flowchart

What happens when a GST is submitted

Read top-down. The main path is a supplier edit; two self-contained sub-flows hang off it with dotted links:

  1. Format gate — 15 characters and a valid GSTIN checksum, else rejected on the spot.
  2. Lock gate — if the existing GST is already Tactical or OTP verified, a normal supplier is blocked (fraud-prevention, not a bug). Only a permissioned GLADMIN or a fresh OTP re-proof gets through.
  3. Save & verify — record is saved, PAN is auto-extracted, and a background check hits the Government GST database.
  4. Outcome — pass ⇒ Tactical Verified + legal status derived + success email; needs-review ⇒ manual queue; fail ⇒ rejected, supplier can resubmit.
  5. Nightly cron re-checks recently-changed and previously-rejected GSTs with no email. HSN mapping is an admin-only flow that links tax-classification codes to a GST and notifies every account on it.

Where GST data moves across processes and stores

Seven processes cover every flow (A–E from the technical doc):

  • 1.0 Validate & save reads the lock state from D2, writes the registration record to D1, and publishes the change onto the queue.
  • 2.0 Verify (async consumer) calls the Govt/BI API, writes verification state to D2, and triggers the success/reject email.
  • 3.0 Fan-out keeps D1 and the D2 replicas in sync; 4.0 derives legal status.
  • 5.0 HSN mapping upserts D3, writes an audit row to D4, and notifies accounts via the queue.
  • 6.0 Nightly cron scans the BigQuery mirrors (D5), republishes GLIDs, and alerts Google Chat on failure. 7.0 serves all reads.

Sequence diagram · Flow A

GST submit & verify, call by call

The synchronous API path is short; the real work is asynchronous:

  1. The API derives PAN, runs regex + checksum, then reads src_id for the lock-check.
  2. alt — if already verified, the caller gets a 200 business error and nothing is written.
  3. Otherwise the registration row is written, a message is published, and the supplier gets 200 OK immediately.
  4. The consumer runs three things in parallel: MAIN-DB update, the BI verification call + GLUSR_GST_DETAILS write, and a conditional RTF upsert.
  5. alt — any goroutine failure republishes to USER_GST_REPROCESS; on full success the outcome email is sent.

Glossary

Business terms that appear across the diagrams and explanations above. Verification states map to the internal FK_GST_VERIFICATION_SRC_ID code shown in brackets.

Verification states & locking

Unverified / freshly submitted (src 1)
GST has just been entered, or auto-matched at a basic level. The supplier can still edit it freely.
Tactical Verified (src 2)
The system automatically matched the GST against the external Government / BI GST database. The GST is now locked — a normal supplier can no longer edit it; only a permissioned GLADMIN or a fresh OTP re-proof can.
OTP Verified (src 3)
The supplier personally proved ownership of the GST with a one-time password. Strongest lock — this proof can override even a rejected GST.
Rejected (src 4)
The GST failed verification or did not match the Government database. The supplier may submit a corrected GST; the nightly cron also re-checks these automatically.
Manual Verification (src 5)
The system could not decide automatically. A human reviewer must confirm or reject the GST.
GST lock
The business rule that a Tactical- or OTP-verified GST cannot be changed through normal profile editing. It is a fraud-prevention feature, not a bug — it stops someone copying a trusted supplier's GST onto their own account.
OTP re-proof
Re-submitting a one-time password to prove GST ownership. The one route (besides a permissioned GLADMIN) that unlocks a locked or rejected GST.

Tax & identity terms

GSTIN / GST number
The 15-character Goods & Services Tax Identification Number issued by the Indian government. It encodes the state code, the holder's PAN, and the entity type.
GSTIN checksum
A mathematical check digit built into the GST number, validated independently of the text format so that typos and fabricated numbers are caught.
PAN
Permanent Account Number — the income-tax ID. It sits inside characters 3–12 of the GSTIN, so IndiaMART extracts it automatically instead of asking the supplier. Once the GST is verified, PAN is locked too.
CIN
Corporate Identification Number — the registration number for an incorporated company.
TAN
Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number — required for businesses that deduct tax at source.
IEC
Import Export Code — needed to import or export goods. Re-checked by the nightly cron alongside GST.
Udyam
The government's MSME (micro / small / medium enterprise) registration number.
HSN
Harmonized System of Nomenclature — the tax-classification code for what a supplier sells. Linked to a GST through the HSN-mapping flow so catalog and tax reporting line up.
Legal Status / Company Type
Whether the business is a Proprietorship, Partnership / LLP, Private Limited Company, HUF, Trust, etc. Derived automatically from one character of the GSTIN rather than asked, so a supplier cannot inflate it.
Nature of Business
What the business does — manufacturer, wholesaler, retailer, service provider. Returned by the Government / BI verification and stored on the GST record.
Annual Turnover
The yearly-revenue band returned by the Government / BI verification.

People & teams

Supplier
The IndiaMART seller who submits or edits their GST from the seller panel or app.
GLID
GlobalID — the unique internal identifier for one user / company account.
GLADMIN
An internal IndiaMART admin user. With an explicit screen permission, a GLADMIN can override a locked GST — otherwise even they are blocked.
BI GST Team
IndiaMART's internal Business Intelligence team that runs the system which cross-verifies GSTs against the government database and feeds results back.
Government GST Verification System
The external, government-backed system that confirms whether a GST is valid and returns real details — turnover, nature of business, registration year, legal status.

Systems & processes

Trust status / trust badge
The buyer-facing signal that a supplier is a real, tax-registered business. It improves once the GST is verified. Separate from star ratings — a high rating does not mean the GST is verified.
TACT re-verification cron
The nightly automated job that re-checks GSTs (and PAN / CIN / IEC / Udyam) changed in the last 24 hours, plus previously-rejected ones. Runs silently — it never emails the supplier.
Consumer
A background worker that processes queued messages asynchronously, after the API has already responded to the supplier.
Fan-out
Propagating one GST change to every database that keeps a copy — the registration record, the verification record, and its trust-database replica.
Reprocess / retry queue
Where a partially-failed GST update is re-queued, so the change eventually lands in every database rather than being left half-applied.
RTF (Real Time Feed)
A generic internal framework for pushing live data updates to downstream systems. The GST flow reuses it conditionally.
Audit trail
A record of who changed what and when — written for every HSN mapping add or remove.
Registration-screen exemption
A specific internal onboarding screen is trusted to submit GST values without the usual format and checksum checks.
